Dirty-data headline. OHLC geometry is clean: 0 high<low, 0 non-positive, 0 open/close outside [low,high] (contrast the VN panels, which carry geometry violations). The real issues are flat/stale historical stretches: 50,475 zero-range (H==L) days (1.15% of rows) and 49,521 days inside ≥5-day identical-close runs (1.13%), concentrated in pre-2000 low-resolution bars and pre-spinoff synthetic series. These drive the zero-Parkinson (floored) targets: mean per-ticker zero-target fraction 1.126%, up to 80.1% for the worst names (SW, FERG, AMCR). 104 extreme single-day moves (|log-return|>50%) across 72 tickers, dominated by the Oct-1987 crash and a few pre-1990 prints (data is adjusted, so these are not splits). Zero-volume days: 0.57% of rows.
Cross-sectional structure. On the most-recent 504 trading days (501 names), mean off-diagonal |ρ| = 0.216 with a strongly POSITIVE common factor (mean signed ρ = 0.204; 3.3% of pairs >0.5). Every S&P 500 name co-moves with one market factor.
Top NEW findings. (1) Correlation MAGNITUDE alone does not explain where graph/deep models helped: the curated large-cap VN30/VN100 are actually MORE correlated (|ρ|≈0.33-0.35) than the broad S&P 500 (0.216), yet the broad 500-node panel is where deep/graph lifted. The driver is node count × liquidity × estimable stable cross-sectional edges, not average ρ. (2) The S&P 500's dirty data is a historical-resolution problem (flat pre-2000 bars), orthogonal to VN's illiquidity problem (live H==L limit days) — same zero-Parkinson symptom, different cause, different fix. (3) The liquidity+zero screen legitimately removes SW (80% zero-target) and FERG (62%), explaining the ~498 screened universe.

4. Cross-sectional structure

The heatmap is overwhelmingly positive (red) — a single dominant market factor. Mean signed ρ = 0.204 vs mean |ρ| = 0.216 (nearly equal ⇒ almost no negatively-correlated pairs). This dense, stable, positive cross-sectional block is exactly the structure a graph/spatial model can exploit — and there are ~500 nodes with long liquid histories to estimate edges from, unlike the thin VN exchanges.

5. Temporal structure

The Parkinson-variance ACF decays slowly (strong volatility clustering, the property HAR exploits). market_pk shows clear regime spikes: 1998, 2000-02, 2008-09, and the 2020 COVID shock. The zero-range fraction falls sharply after ~2000 as historical bar resolution improves — early-decade illiquidity/low-resolution is a data-vintage artifact, not live market illiquidity.

Why graph/deep models behave differently across universes.
A spatial/graph model needs three things at once: (a) MANY nodes, (b) LIQUID, long histories so cross-sectional edges are estimable and stable, and (c) genuine shared structure. Ranking:
  • S&P 500 — ~498 liquid nodes, decades of history, a strong positive common factor (|ρ|=0.22, almost all positive). Average ρ is only moderate, but there is enough data to learn stable edges ⇒ deep/graph capacity pays off. Matches the prior finding that deep/graph lifted most on the large S&P 500 panel.
  • VN30 / VN100 — HIGHER average |ρ| (0.33-0.35) but only 33/104 nodes and the co-movement is a single market factor already captured by market_pk, so a graph adds little; few nodes also means little to spatially pool.
  • HOSE — broad (403) but moderate ρ (0.17) and mixed liquidity.
  • HNX — thinnest: |ρ|=0.08 (mostly idiosyncratic noise) and heavy illiquidity (live H==L limit days). Edges estimated here are noise; a graph cannot help and floored zero-target days dominate QLIKE.
The dirty-data cause differs too: S&P 500 zero-targets come from HISTORICAL low-resolution / pre-spinoff flat bars (fixable by a start-date/vintage screen); VN zero-targets come from LIVE illiquidity (H==L limit-lock days) — market microstructure, not a vintage artifact. Same symptom, different cause, different remedy.
