Most important findings

  1. Extreme illiquidity dominates the target. 45.3% of all ticker-days have Parkinson variance EXACTLY zero (H=L limit/no-trade days). These are floored in QLIKE and make the volatility target degenerate for many names — the single biggest data caveat.
  2. The liquidity screen removes 137/299 tickers. Only 162 survive (>= 250 rows and <= 50% zero-Parkinson days); the raw HNX universe is far larger than the usable one.
  3. Cross-sectional structure is weak. Median pairwise |return correlation| = 0.094; only 1/13,038 pairs exceed 0.7. A spatial graph over HNX returns has little signal to exploit.
  4. 3467 OHLC-geometry violations cluster on specific dates (see seam table) — open/close outside [low,high] recurring across many tickers on the same day indicates a data-source artifact, not independent per-ticker errors.
  5. Fat tails + volatility clustering are present. Return excess kurtosis = 102.1; |return| ACF stays positive over weeks (see chart) — standard for a real equity market and consistent with HAR being a strong baseline.

1. Coverage & structure

Each ticker enters on its listing year; the panel is an unbalanced (staggered) panel with heavy left-censoring.

2. Distributions

Return skew=-0.37, excess-kurt=102.1; Parkinson-variance skew=9.91, excess-kurt=327.1. Parkinson/volume/HAR are shown on log10 x (heavy right tails; zeros dropped in log view).

4. Cross-sectional structure

Median |corr| = 0.094; fraction of pairs >0.7 = 0.0001. Weak co-movement implies a return-correlation graph adds little cross-stock information — consistent with the project's negative graph-ablation result.

5. Temporal patterns

Slowly-decaying positive autocorrelation of |returns| = volatility clustering; the zero-Parkinson-by-year chart (section 3a) also shows the illiquidity regime shrinking over time as the exchange matured.
