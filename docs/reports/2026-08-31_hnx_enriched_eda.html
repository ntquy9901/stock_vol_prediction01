The ~45% zero-range mass means the parkinson variance target is a spike-at-zero mixture. QLIKE = y/ŷ − log(y/ŷ) − 1 is dominated by the positivity floor on these days (tiny ŷ or floored y drive the ratio), so HNX vol-forecast QLIKE is floor-sensitive rather than skill-sensitive — consistent with prior findings that low-range HNX days are not forecastable and the remedy is a floor/liquidity screen, not a better estimator.

Note — why ~45% zero-range makes HNX a hard target

On roughly 45% of HNX ticker-days the high==low (zero intraday range), so the Parkinson estimator (σ² ∝ log(H/L)²) is exactly 0. The forecasting target is therefore a spike-at-zero mixture rather than a smooth positive process. Two consequences: (1) any model's QLIKE is governed by the positivity floor applied to zero/near-zero targets and predictions, making the metric floor-sensitive rather than skill-sensitive; (2) the persistent, forecastable component (HAR memory) exists only on the minority of liquid days, so lift over HAR is small and fragile. This matches prior project findings that HNX low-range days are not forecastable, that the correct remedy is a floor / liquidity screen (not a different estimator), and that HNX-wide GNN edges fail to beat a no-graph baseline on thin returns.
