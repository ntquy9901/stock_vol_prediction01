# Analysis of arXiv 2512.08567v1 and Applicability to the Track-B VN30 Volatility Model

Source: `docs/paper/2512.08567v1.pdf` (11 pages, read-only). Page/section citations refer to the
PDF text as extracted.

## 1. Bibliographic and framing

- **Title:** "A Hybrid Model for Stock Market Forecasting: Integrating News Sentiment and Time
  Series Data with Graph Neural Networks" (p.1).
- **Authors:** Nader Sadek, Mirette Moawad, Christina Naguib, Mariam Elzahaby — Faculty of
  Computers and Artificial Intelligence, Cairo University, Egypt (p.1).
- **Venue/date:** Proceedings of the 5th International Conference on AI Research (ICAIR 2025),
  pp.452–462. Undergraduate/faculty project (Acknowledgments, p.10: supervised project, no external
  funding).
- **Problem solved:** Predict the **direction** of next-day stock price movement (up/down) by fusing
  financial news with historical price. Objective is explicitly to show a GNN beats an LSTM baseline
  (p.5, §5).
- **Domain — critical mismatch:** This is a **price-direction classification** paper, **not a
  volatility paper**. Target is binary sign of `close_t − close_{t-1}` (p.3, §3.1). It uses **daily
  close prices only** and **large US universes** (325 and 625 companies). It does **not** model
  volatility, does **not** use HAR/GARCH, and does **not** do regression.

## 2. Core method

- **Node definition (heterogeneous graph):** three node types — company, industry, article
  (p.5, §4.2). Article node features = news embeddings (Sigma or FinBERT). Company node features =
  concatenation of LSTM stock embedding + a learnable embedding. Industry node features = learnable
  embedding only.
- **Edge / graph construction:** edges come from **news co-mention**, not from price correlation.
  Each article node connects to its "main company" plus other companies mentioned in the article;
  each company connects to its industry (p.5, §4.2). Graph is rebuilt over a 15-day window before the
  target day. For the Bloomberg dataset the industry node is dropped and articles are split into
  sentences before encoding (p.5, §4.2). Edges are unweighted co-occurrence; no correlation, no
  learned/dynamic adjacency, no causal/spillover edges, no hypergraph.
- **Temporal component:** 2-layer LSTM over a 15-day sequence of the close price (p.5, §4.3); one
  experiment substitutes a Transformer (Table 2, Exp 6). A batch-norm layer precedes the LSTM;
  authors state raw prices were left un-normalized to avoid leakage across split strategies (p.5).
- **Graph + temporal fusion:** LSTM activations are used directly as company-node embeddings; the
  LSTM is trained **jointly, end-to-end** with the GNN (p.5, §4.3). Message passing is
  **GraphSAGE**, 3 GNN layers (Table 1, p.6).
- **Objective / target:** binary classification (cross-entropy implied; loss not stated). Two target
  definitions (p.3–4, §3.1): (T1) plain direction up/down; (T2) "significance" target — 1 only if the
  move exceeds `0.04 × std(close_{t-N:t})` with N=100, i.e. a volatility-adjusted threshold. T2 makes
  the data imbalanced, so precision is the headline metric for T2.

## 3. GNN improvements / contributions claimed

The paper introduces **no new message-passing operator, no attention variant, no graph-structure
learning, no positional encoding, no regularization scheme**. Its claimed contributions are
design/integration choices and empirical insights (p.3 §2.2 gap statement; p.5–7). Enumerated:

1. **News co-mention heterogeneous graph as the relational signal** — the central claim: define
   inter-company edges from *news co-mention* while using price as node features, within one GNN
   (gap statement, p.3 §2.2; construction p.5 §4.2). Mechanism: information from an article and from
   co-mentioned companies propagates to the target company. Claimed to help because it injects
   external event context that price-only graphs miss. Evidence: Table 3 (p.8) GNN 53% vs LSTM 52%
   accuracy (T1); precision 55.5% vs 51% (T2); recall markedly higher (0.72 vs 0.63; 0.02 vs 0.003).
2. **Joint end-to-end LSTM+GNN training** (p.5 §4.3). Mechanism: LSTM company embeddings are learned
   through the GNN loss. No isolated ablation of "joint vs frozen"; presented as design.
3. **GraphSAGE chosen over GAT for a heterophilic graph** (p.7 §5.1.3, Exp 3). Mechanism/insight:
   co-mentioned companies do not share the same label, so the graph is heterophilic; GAT's
   attention assumes homophily and degrades (precision collapse to 46%/25% on T2), while GraphSAGE
   neighborhood sampling is more robust. Evidence: single ablation row (Exp 3) plus citations
   (Zhu 2020, Pei 2020).
4. **Learnable node embeddings** for company/industry concatenated with encoded features (p.5 §4.2).
   Presented as design, not ablated.
5. **Headlines-only news encoding beats full article** (p.7 §5.1.3; Exp 1 vs 2). Precision 55% (title)
   vs 53.4% (content). Framed as signal-to-noise finding; recommends event extraction (p.10 §6.1).
6. **Domain-specific news encoder (Sigma vs FinBERT)** (p.7 §5.1.3; Exp 2 vs 4). Sigma marginally
   better (55% vs 54% precision).
7. **Significance-thresholded target + top-confidence company subsetting** (p.4 §3.1; p.6 §5.1).
   Metrics reported on the 100/200 highest-confidence companies to surface where the graph helps.

## 4. Results and rigor

- **Headline results (Table 3, p.8):** GNN vs LSTM — T1 accuracy 0.53 vs 0.52 (+1 pt), F1 0.61 vs
  0.57; T2 precision 0.555 vs 0.51 (+~4 pts), recall 0.02 vs 0.003. Best config = Exp 2 (GraphSAGE +
  Sigma + LSTM close + article title).
- **Datasets:** US equities (82k articles, 325 companies, 2015–2020; 1100 train / 175 test days) and
  Bloomberg (450k articles, 625 companies, 2006–2013) (p.4 §3.2). **Large universes.**
- **Baselines:** only a **close-price LSTM**. No HAR, no GARCH, no naive/persistence, no strong ML
  baseline. (Not applicable in the usual volatility sense, but there is also no rigorous direction
  baseline such as momentum.)
- **Significance testing — a real gap:** the only test (p.7 §5.1.4) is a one-sample proportion
  Z-test of accuracy against **random chance p=0.50** (Z=14.31). This tests "better than a coin,"
  **not** "better than the LSTM baseline." The abstract's phrase "statistically significant 1%
  improvement over the baseline" (p.1) is **not** supported by any test comparing GNN vs LSTM. No
  Diebold-Mariano, no MCS, no paired test on the 1-pt gap. Out-of-sample split is chronological
  (train/test days) which is appropriate, but with a single split and no CI on the gap.
- **Honesty caveats the authors do state:** the 1% gain is "diluted" because news-poor companies
  default the GNN to LSTM-level performance; gains concentrate in news-rich names (p.8 §5.2, Fig.3).
  They flag textual noise, a simplistic co-mention schema, and absence of calibrated uncertainty
  (p.10 §6.1–6.3). These are candid but also mean the reported edge is thin and conditional on news
  density.

**Overall rigor assessment:** weak. A ~1-pt accuracy gain over a single weak baseline, no paired
significance test of that gain, one train/test split, and a self-reported dependence on news
density. This paper is not evidence that a graph beats a strong econometric volatility baseline.

## 5. Applicability to the Track-B VN30 volatility model

Our regime: daily OHLCV, 33 VN30 tickers (small, news-sparse Vietnamese market), target = 5-day
Parkinson **variance** (regression), pooled ~73k ticker-day samples, node = 3 HAR scales + PhoBERT
news, per-ticker gate, masked k-NN-8 **correlation** graph over a pooled LSTM (G1). Established
finding: the graph is **null** — G1 ties HAR at all horizons, DM n.s.; already-failed attempts to
beat HAR include QLIKE-loss, HAR+graph-residual decomposition, directed Diebold-Yilmaz spillover
edges, learned/dynamic MTGNN adjacency, omit-self-loops (`docs/reports/2026-08-10_0412_beat_har_
sweep_results.md`). Literature review (`2026-08-09_2209`, `..._2214`) established that daily/
small-universe GNNs rarely beat HAR; documented wins need intraday RV or large universe.

Three structural mismatches dominate the assessment: (i) the paper targets **direction
classification**, we target **variance regression** — its metric gains do not transfer; (ii) the
paper's graph win is defined against a **close-price LSTM**, never against **HAR/GARCH**, so it
offers zero evidence about our actual null (graph vs HAR); (iii) the paper's mechanism is **news-
co-mention edges on a large universe**, and its own results say the effect is carried by **news-rich
companies** — the opposite of the news-sparse 33-ticker VN30 daily regime.

### Bottom line

The paper provides **no technique that plausibly moves our graph off the null**, and it is weak
evidence for our question because (a) it is a direction-classification paper, not volatility; (b) its
"win" is a ~1-pt accuracy gain over a close-price LSTM with **no paired significance test** and **no
HAR/GARCH comparison**; (c) its mechanism and gains depend on **large, news-rich universes** we do
not have. The single genuinely new idea relative to our sweep is **news-co-mention edges** (items in
our sweep were all price/correlation-derived); it is the only candidate worth a bounded probe, but
given VN30's tiny, news-sparse daily regime and the paper's own admission that the effect vanishes
for news-poor names, it is expected to be another null rather than a lift. It does not contradict the
established conclusion that daily/small-universe graphs do not beat HAR.
