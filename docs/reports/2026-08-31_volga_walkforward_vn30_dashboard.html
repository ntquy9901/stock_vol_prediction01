1. How the data is organised over time (train / validation / forecast)

The panel is evaluated with an expanding-window walk-forward scheme, not a single fixed train/test cut. Time runs left to right along the trading-day axis; each row below is one fold. Every fold trains on all history up to an anchor, holds out the last 66 rows for validation (early stopping), then forecasts the next K=16 trading days out-of-sample; the window then expands by K days and the model is retrained. This repeats for 22 folds (monthly retrain). The vol→PK graph and every per-node feature/target scaler are estimated on the train window only and frozen for validation and forecast, so no future information leaks into any fold.

6. Error-metric interpretation

QLIKE is the primary loss for this task: it is scale-invariant and tail-sensitive, which suits variance targets whose magnitude varies across stocks and regimes. RMSE, MAE and R2 are secondary point-error metrics; RMSE and MSE weight large errors quadratically, MAE weights them linearly, and R2 rescales MSE against target variance (so its ranking matches MSE and RMSE by construction).

Ranking consistency on VN30: the QLIKE-best model coincides with the RMSE-best and MAE-best model at none. The ranking flips between metrics at h1 (QLIKE favors HAR, RMSE VolGA, MAE VolGA); h5 (QLIKE favors HAR-X, RMSE LSTM, MAE LSTM); h10 (QLIKE favors LSTM, RMSE LSTM, MAE VolGA); h22 (QLIKE favors HAR-X, RMSE HAR-X, MAE LSTM). Where the ranking flips, the point-error metrics (RMSE, MAE, MSE, R2) tend to favour the deep models, whose predictions reduce mean and tail point error, while QLIKE can still favour an econometric baseline because it penalises proportional (multiplicative) error rather than absolute error. The DM tables in Section 5 make this explicit: on some pairs and horizons the squared-error and absolute-error DM tests reach significance while the QLIKE DM test does not, so the marginal-value verdict is loss-function-dependent and is reported on all three bases rather than QLIKE alone.

10. Objective verdict

  • Graph marginal value (VolGA vs no-graph LSTM, QLIKE DM): not statistically significant at any horizon (h1 p=0.179, h5 p=0.112, h10 p=0.265, h22 p=0.928).
  • On QLIKE the marginal-value verdict can differ from the squared-error and absolute-error verdicts; see the full DM tables (Section 5) for the per-basis, per-horizon detail.
  • Point QLIKE winners by horizon: h1 HAR, h5 HAR-X, h10 LSTM, h22 HAR-X. A deep model attains the lowest pooled QLIKE at h10.
  • Deep vs econometric: VolGA vs HAR-X (QLIKE DM) p-values h1 0.157, h5 0.397, h10 0.717, h22 0.788; no deep model significantly beats HAR-X on QLIKE at any horizon.
  • Directional accuracy is not part of these results (not computed by the walk-forward suite; unreliable for variance targets due to anti-persistence).
