\title{Deep Sequence Models versus HAR for Daily Volatility Forecasting:
A Per-Observation Study with a Graph-Attention Ablation on Vietnamese and U.S. Equities}

\author{Author One\inst{1} \and Author Two\inst{1}}
\authorrunning{Author One et al.}
\institute{Affiliation, City, Country \\ \email{\{author1,author2\}@example.edu}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We study whether a deep sequence model can improve daily Parkinson-variance forecasting over the
Heterogeneous Autoregressive (HAR) baseline, and whether a cross-sectional graph-attention branch helps.
Our main model is a pooled per-node LSTM over three HAR features (daily, weekly, monthly Parkinson
volatility), evaluated against two classical baselines to beat, HAR and GARCH(1,1), under a
per-observation per-stock chronological 80/10/10 split with five seeds, MSE training loss and
validation-MSE early stopping. On the Vietnamese markets the LSTM beats HAR at the one-day horizon by the
Diebold--Mariano QLIKE test (VN30 and VN100 significant; VN100 also at one week), and every learned model
beats GARCH. As a controlled leave-one-out ablation we add a Graph Attention Network over a graphical-lasso
partial-correlation graph (LSTM+GAT), which requires a common-date snapshot design; removing the graph
improves QLIKE in all eight snapshot configurations, so the graph does not help. We further show the
deep-versus-HAR verdict is sensitive to the data design: the same LSTM loses to HAR under a common-date
snapshot with a global split but wins under the per-observation split. Deep-versus-HAR competitiveness also
grows with panel size, replicating from Vietnam to the S\&P~500. A longer lookback (22 vs 10) gives no
benefit. All numbers are read from stored results; we report the negative graph ablation and the QLIKE/MSE
disagreements honestly.
\keywords{Volatility forecasting \and HAR \and LSTM \and Graph Attention Network \and Diebold--Mariano.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Daily volatility forecasting underpins risk management, position sizing and derivative pricing. The HAR
model of Corsi~\cite{corsi2009} is a strong, parsimonious benchmark: three OLS coefficients on daily,
weekly and monthly aggregates capture much of the long-memory structure. Deep sequence models and graph
neural networks are frequently proposed to capture nonlinear temporal dynamics and cross-sectional
spillovers, but the evidence that they beat HAR out of sample is mixed and positive claims are often
confounded by data-design choices.

We ask two questions, with the Vietnamese equity market as the primary case study and the U.S. S\&P~500 as
a robustness check: (1) Can a pooled LSTM over the three HAR features beat HAR and GARCH out of sample, and
at which horizons? (2) Does adding a cross-sectional Graph Attention Network branch, with edges estimated by
graphical lasso, improve the LSTM? Our contributions are: (i) a fair per-observation per-stock evaluation in
which the LSTM significantly beats HAR at the short horizon on both Vietnamese markets; (ii) a clean
leave-one-out ablation showing the graph-attention branch consistently fails to help; (iii) a methodological
finding that the deep-versus-HAR verdict flips with the data design; and (iv) a data-scaling observation,
consistent across Vietnam and the U.S., that deep models grow more competitive with HAR as the panel grows.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{HAR and classical models.} HAR~\cite{corsi2009} approximates the long memory of realized volatility
with a daily/weekly/monthly cascade; recent work reports that rolling-window and specification choices,
rather than model class, often drive apparent gains over HAR~\cite{audrino2025}. HARQ~\cite{harq2016}
exploits measurement error, and GARCH-family models remain standard conditional-variance benchmarks. We use
HAR and GARCH(1,1) as the baselines to beat.
\textbf{Deep and graph models.} LSTMs~\cite{lstm1997} are widely applied to financial series, and graph
neural networks model cross-asset spillovers, e.g.\ Graph Attention Networks~\cite{gat2018}, multivariate
realized-volatility GNNs~\cite{chen2022} and spillover-aware GNN-HAR hybrids~\cite{gnnhar2025}; evidence is
mixed. \textbf{Graph construction.} Rather than a market-factor-dominated correlation graph, we estimate a
sparse partial-correlation graph with graphical lasso~\cite{glasso2008} on training rows only.
\textbf{Evaluation.} Volatility is latent, so we use the proxy-robust QLIKE loss~\cite{patton2011} alongside
MSE, with significance by the Diebold--Mariano test~\cite{dm1995} and the Harvey--Leybourne--Newbold
small-sample correction~\cite{hln1997}.

\section{Method}
\textbf{Target and features.} The target is the Parkinson variance~\cite{parkinson1980} at day $t{+}h$ for
$h\in\{1,5\}$, a non-negative point forecast. Following HAR, three features per node are used: the daily
Parkinson variance at $t$, its 5-day rolling mean (weekly) and its 22-day rolling mean (monthly). The same
features drive every model, so differences reflect functional form, not the information set.

\textbf{Main model: per-observation LSTM.} The main model (``LSTM'') is a two-layer LSTM (hidden 64) over the
lookback window of the three features; a single pooled model is trained over all tickers, each standardized
by its own scaler fit on training rows only, with a linear output inverse-transformed for evaluation.
Overfitting is controlled by dropout, weight decay, gradient clipping, a ReduceLROnPlateau schedule and early
stopping. Training examples are formed by \emph{per-observation pooling}: every (ticker, window) pair is an
independent example and the chronological 80/10/10 split is applied within each ticker's own history,
yielding tens of thousands of windows and interleaving each ticker's regimes.

\textbf{Graph variant: LSTM+GAT (ablation).} To test whether a cross-sectional graph helps, a Graph Attention
Network branch~\cite{gat2018} is added (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}): a per-node LSTM encodes the temporal window,
and a GAT reads the node features at $t$ and attends over a fixed Top-5 graphical-lasso partial-correlation
graph estimated on training rows only; the branches are concatenated into an MLP head. The leave-one-out
``LSTM (w/o GAT)'' removes the spatial branch. A GAT operates over a common-date cross-section, so LSTM+GAT
requires a \emph{common-date snapshot} design (only dates on which all nodes are present) with a single
global 80/10/10 split; we thus report the graph ablation as a separate study to avoid confounding.

\textbf{Baselines.} HAR is a pooled OLS on the three features refit on the same training rows; GARCH(1,1) is
a per-ticker conditional-variance model.

\textbf{Protocol.} All models train with MSE loss and select the checkpoint by validation MSE (QLIKE is not
used for training or selection, per specification). Each configuration runs five seeds
$\{42,123,2026,7,2024\}$, up to 20 epochs with early stopping. We report MSE, RMSE, MAE, QLIKE and $R^2$,
seed-averaged, with a shared QLIKE floor $10^{-8}$; significance uses Diebold--Mariano (HLN correction, HAC
lag $h{-}1$) on per-observation QLIKE and squared-error losses. A negative statistic favors the deep model.

\section{Data}
Three panels are used with the Parkinson target and three HAR features: \textbf{VN30} (33 tickers, primary,
Vietnam), \textbf{VN100} (104, vnstock), and \textbf{S\&P~500} ($\sim$500, U.S. robustness; Yahoo-sourced,
only aggregate metrics stored). The main study uses per-ticker 80/10/10 splits and a pooled model; the graph
study uses common-date fixed-node snapshots with a global split. The S\&P~500 list is current membership, so
U.S. levels carry survivorship bias; we claim the short-versus-long ordering transfers, not absolute levels.

\section{Experiments}
We run four studies on the same features, MSE loss and validation-MSE selection: (1) \emph{Main}: LSTM vs HAR
and GARCH on the per-observation design (VN30, VN100, S\&P~500; lookback 10; $h\in\{1,5\}$); (2)
\emph{Graph-check}: LSTM+GAT vs LSTM (w/o GAT) on the snapshot design; (3) \emph{Lookback} 10 vs 22 (snapshot,
VN30); (4) \emph{Cross-market / data scaling} across the three panels.

\section{Results}
Parkinson variance is of order $10^{-7}$; MSE, RMSE, MAE are scaled (MSE $\times10^{-7}$, RMSE/MAE
$\times10^{-4}$). Row order is HAR $\to$ GARCH $\to$ LSTM (and, for the graph study, adding LSTM (w/o GAT)
$\to$ LSTM+GAT). All values are five-seed test means.

\subsection{Main study --- per-observation LSTM}
Table~\ref{tab:main} reports QLIKE and the Diebold--Mariano verdicts (full 5-metric tables omitted for space
follow the same ordering). On QLIKE the LSTM significantly beats HAR at $h1$ on both Vietnamese panels and at

\subsection{Lookback variation and data scaling}
On VN30 the longer lookback (22 vs 10) does not help: LSTM (w/o GAT) QLIKE is essentially unchanged
(0.4137 vs 0.4120 at $h1$) and LSTM+GAT is slightly worse (0.4714 vs 0.4528); the graph again hurts
(DM $+8.80$ at $h1$). Reading the main-study QLIKE across panels of increasing size shows a consistent
gradient: the LSTM's edge over HAR is modest and $h1$-only on VN30, significant at both horizons on VN100,
and dominates HAR on MSE/$R^2$ on the S\&P~500. An independent per-ticker 70/15/15 run with validation-QLIKE
early stopping recovered a QLIKE win at both horizons on the S\&P~500, confirming the U.S. QLIKE ties here
are a model-selection artifact rather than a market effect.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:disc}
\textbf{Data design drives the verdict.} The same LSTM wins against HAR under the per-observation per-stock
design but loses under the common-date snapshot with a global split. The intersection keeps only all-nodes-
present dates, shrinking VN30 from tens of thousands of windows to $\sim$1000 common dates, and a single
global split places the test set in one recent regime; both starve and regime-shift the deep model
(validation MSE exceeds the standardized-mean baseline of~1). The per-observation design removes both
handicaps. This explains apparently contradictory ``deep beats/loses to HAR'' claims.
\textbf{Loss and selection matter.} Training and early stopping use MSE, but QLIKE upweights low-volatility
days; on the S\&P~500 the LSTM wins on MSE but not QLIKE at $h1$ purely because the checkpoint is chosen by
validation MSE. We keep validation-MSE selection to honor the specification and report the disagreement
transparently. \textbf{The graph adds noise, not signal.} Across all eight snapshot configurations and both
lookbacks, removing the GAT improves QLIKE --- a clean negative ablation. \textbf{Data scaling.}
Competitiveness grows with panel size from VN30 to VN100 to the S\&P~500, suggesting HAR's edge on small
Vietnamese panels is a small-sample phenomenon.

\section{Limitations}
Model selection is by validation MSE (not QLIKE), producing QLIKE/MSE disagreements we report rather than
cherry-pick. The graph is only evaluable on the data-poorer snapshot design, though the leave-one-out
ablation is valid within-design. GAT attention is quadratic in nodes (small batch needed at $\sim$500
nodes). The S\&P~500 list is current membership (survivorship bias); we claim only the ordering and scaling
transfer. All numbers are read from stored results; no result was fabricated.

\section{Conclusion}
A pooled per-observation LSTM over three HAR features significantly beats HAR at the one-day horizon on both
Vietnamese markets (and one week on VN100), while every learned model beats GARCH. Adding a Graph Attention
Network over a graphical-lasso graph does not help: removing it improves QLIKE in all eight snapshot
configurations. The deep-versus-HAR verdict is sensitive to the data design, and competitiveness grows with
panel size, replicating from Vietnam to the S\&P~500. A longer lookback yields no benefit. We recommend a
graph-free per-observation HAR-LSTM for short-horizon volatility, with HAR a strong baseline hardest to beat
on small panels and at long horizons.
